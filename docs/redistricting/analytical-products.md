# Analytical Products

## Purpose

This document defines the reports, dashboards, and reusable outputs that the redistricting audit module should produce.

The audience may include analysts, journalists, civic organizations, legal teams, campaigns, academic researchers, and the public. Outputs should be transparent, reproducible, and careful about uncertainty.

## Product 1 — Redistricting plan audit report

### Purpose

Evaluate one enacted or proposed plan against neutral and objective-driven simulations.

### Sections

1. Executive summary.
2. Plan metadata.
3. Legal and state-rule context.
4. Population equality and contiguity checks.
5. District demographic summary.
6. Election composite summary.
7. Competitiveness summary.
8. Partisan fairness metrics.
9. Minority-opportunity screening.
10. County / municipality / community splits.
11. Incumbent impact.
12. Ensemble outlier analysis.
13. Hypothesis-fit analysis.
14. Stress-test results.
15. Caveats and data limitations.

### Key charts

- enacted map vs neutral ensemble seat distribution;
- district margin histogram;
- seats-votes curve;
- competitiveness bands;
- county and municipality split maps;
- minority CVAP distribution;
- enacted map vs partisan-hypothesis ensemble similarity.

## Product 3 — Competitiveness opportunity report

### Purpose

Show whether more competitive elections were feasible under constraints.

### Key questions

- How many competitive districts does the enacted plan create?
- How many competitive districts do neutral maps typically create?
- How many competitive districts are feasible under a competitiveness-seeking objective?
- What tradeoffs arise with compactness, community preservation, and minority opportunity?

### Outputs

- competitive-district count distribution;
- map examples from neutral and competitiveness-seeking ensembles;
- tradeoff frontier: competitiveness vs compactness;
- tradeoff frontier: competitiveness vs minority opportunity;
- tradeoff frontier: competitiveness vs county splits.

## Product 4 — Gerrymander hypothesis-fit report

### Purpose

Test whether an enacted map resembles maps optimized for partisan advantage more than it resembles neutral or public-interest maps.

### Sections

1. Hypothesis definitions.
2. Objective functions used.
3. Constraints held constant.
4. Simulation diagnostics.
5. Similarity scores.
6. District-level mechanism analysis.
7. Sensitivity tests.
8. Conclusion with confidence bands and caveats.

### Core argument pattern

```text
The enacted plan is not merely favorable to Party A. It is structurally similar to maps generated by a Party A advantage objective and is an outlier relative to neutral and competitiveness-seeking ensembles. The similarity is visible in both plan-level seat outcomes and district-level packing/cracking patterns.
```

## Product 5 — Minority representation screening report

### Purpose

Provide an initial representation and dilution-risk screen.

### Important limitation

This product should not present final Voting Rights Act conclusions. It should identify patterns that require legal and expert review.

### Sections

1. Protected-class population summary.
2. VAP and CVAP estimates.
3. Opportunity district screen.
4. Coalition district screen.
5. District-level changes from old to new plan.
6. Comparison with plausible alternative maps.
7. Places where minority communities are packed, cracked, or split.
8. Data limitations and recommended expert review.

## Product 6 — Community split report

### Purpose

Explain whether counties, municipalities, tribal lands, school districts, neighborhoods, or other communities are divided unnecessarily.

### Inputs

- counties;
- places / municipalities;
- school districts;
- tribal geographies;
- neighborhood boundaries, if available;
- public-submission communities of interest;
- commuting zones or mobility communities;
- media markets;
- language communities.

### Outputs

- split-count table;
- split severity score;
- map of split communities;
- comparison with neutral and community-preserving ensembles;
- examples of valid maps with fewer splits.

## Product 7 — Turnout and participation impact report

### Purpose

Explore whether a map plausibly encourages or discourages participation by changing competitiveness and community coherence.

### Important caveat

Turnout response to district lines is uncertain. The report should frame results as hypotheses, not deterministic forecasts.

### Metrics

- share of voters in competitive districts;
- share of CVAP in competitive districts;
- historical turnout in competitive vs noncompetitive districts;
- number of districts where either party has a plausible path;
- turnout-weighted competitiveness;
- community preservation among high-civic-participation areas.

## Product 8 — Public explainer

### Purpose

Make the technical analysis understandable to nontechnical readers.

### Format

- one-page narrative;
- map before / after;
- three headline findings;
- “what changed” table;
- “what the simulations show” chart;
- short caveat box.

### Recommended language

Use:

- “this map is an outlier relative to plausible alternatives”;
- “this map resembles maps optimized for partisan advantage”;
- “this result is robust across several election composites.”

Avoid:

- “the algorithm proved intent”;
- “this is definitely illegal”;
- “compactness alone proves the case.”

## Product 9 — Technical appendix

### Purpose

Make the analysis reproducible.

### Contents

- data inventory;
- acquisition dates;
- source URLs;
- preprocessing scripts;
- allocation assumptions;
- algorithm parameters;
- random seeds;
- validation checks;
- full metric definitions;
- ensemble diagnostics;
- sensitivity tests;
- known limitations.
